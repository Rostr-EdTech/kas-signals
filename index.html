    What do these numbers mean?
    • Times: how many separate occasions the condition was met. Several days in a row count once; a new occasion needs a 20-day gap.
    • +30% within 30d: how often KAS rose at least 30% above that day's price at some point in the next 30 days. It measures the chance of a sharp jump soon after.
    • Median 90d: the typical price change 90 days later. Half the occasions did better, half did worse.
    • Any day (baseline) is the comparison point: a signal is only useful if it beats what a random day would have given you.
    • Tilt vs fixed split: the same money on the same schedule, either always split your usual way (fixed), or shifted to the coin that was cheap vs the other (tilt). The numbers are what each $100 invested became. If both are below $100, the period lost money and the tilt simply lost less. The two halves check that it worked consistently, not just once.

    Few occasions means weak evidence. Most of this history is one long downtrend. More in the guide

    Goal projection

    What this shows (and doesn't)
    • It answers: what would my portfolio be worth on my goal date? Not when you would reach the goal.
    • It starts from your holdings at today's prices, grows each coin at the scenario's steady yearly rate, and adds every regular buy on schedule.
    • It doesn't include real price swings, which are much larger than a steady rate, or your tilts, extra buys and rebalancing.

    How this works (and why to be careful)
    • Each holding grows at its own average yearly rate over the period you pick (1, 2 or 3 years), and your regular KAS/BTC buys are added on schedule.
    • Crypto trends rarely continue: the same coin can show +80%/yr over one period and −40%/yr over another. The line under the table shows your total under all three lookbacks so you can see how much the answer depends on that choice.
    • Coins with shorter history use what's available, marked with *.
    • Big yearly rates compound into huge numbers over several years. Treat this as "what the recent past implies", not a forecast.
